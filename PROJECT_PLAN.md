# Project plan: Research Careers

**Project:** a public, student-owned website about Product Management, Forward Deployed Engineering, and the portfolio evidence that could support either path.  
**Primary direction:** Product Manager (exploratory).  
**Research checked:** November 5, 2026, unless a source capture says otherwise.

## Purpose

Connect the learner's interest in understanding user needs, improving product satisfaction, guiding a team, and connecting people across languages and perspectives to real job descriptions and concrete portfolio work.

The T-shirt brand project is a case study and learning artifact. It is a separate project with its own decisions, evidence, open pull request, and limitations. This site must link to it rather than imply that career research validates the shirt concept.

## Product boundaries

- Keep Product Management the primary career exploration; include Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE) and adjacent AI-enabled work as a comparison, not a forced career choice.
- Use employer job pages for role requirements and the Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS) for adjacent occupational context. Label indirect data clearly; do not present BLS project-management-specialist statistics as a Product Manager outlook.
- Distinguish role requirements from portfolio ideas. Do not claim the learner has skills or experience that the available project does not demonstrate.
- Preserve research dates, source links, role level, and location; job listings and pay can change.
- Clearly label original diagrams and page mockups as visual concepts, not screenshots, employer material, measured results, or market data.
- Keep public personal details limited to the career motivation the learner provided for this site.

## Work sequence

1. Establish the source-backed role notes and project plan.
2. Build the site's page structure and concise research narrative.
3. Add the revision/changelog navigation and labeled original visuals.
4. Publish through GitHub Pages and record the release.
5. Update each issue with its commit and evidence, then close only completed work.

## Initial acceptance checks

- A visitor can understand the career direction and why it interests the learner without reading the repository.
- Each selected job posting includes the date checked, role level/location where visible, responsibilities, skills, and an honest portfolio example.
- BLS figures are explicitly tied to their named occupation and years, with a warning that they are not a Product Manager-specific forecast.
- The portfolio case study links to existing T-shirt research and distinguishes observed facts, hypotheses, and work still pending.
- The site has working navigation, semantic headings, visible keyboard focus, responsive layouts, and no broken local links.
- The plan and change log are reachable from the site navigation.
- Illustrations are labeled concepts, use text equivalents, and do not look like captured employer pages.
- The public Pages URL loads successfully over HTTPS.

## Decisions still open

- Product Manager is the main direction to explore, not a final career commitment.
- The T-shirt project's proposed audience and brand direction remain hypotheses unless supported by real user evidence.
- Future research, job targets, portfolio changes, and releases should be added as dated revisions rather than silently replacing earlier notes.
