**Purpose:** compare actual role expectations with the learner's interests and the work that could be shown in a portfolio.

## Why this direction interests me

I’m exploring Product Management because I like connecting user needs to products that help people get real value and satisfaction. I enjoy guiding people toward a shared goal, connecting ideas and people across different languages and perspectives, and being someone others can turn to for information. I want to learn how PMs turn feedback into product decisions and help a team deliver a product that works well for its users.

This is a career interest, not a final career choice or a claim of professional experience.

## BLS context: related, not a PM forecast

The U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS) Occupational Outlook Handbook reports that **Project Management Specialists (SOC 13-1082)** had a May 2025 median annual wage of **$102,320** and are projected to grow **7% from 2025 to 2035**, with about **76,500 annual openings** on average. The BLS describes these specialists as coordinating project budgets, schedules, staffing, and related work.

Those figures belong to the BLS occupation **Project Management Specialists**. They are useful context for project coordination, but they are **not Product Manager pay or employment projections**. Employer PM titles vary and do not map cleanly to this BLS occupation.

## A practical skill-building path

1. **Listen:** write down the user's situation and the problem before proposing a feature.
2. **Compare:** record what competitor pages actually show and what remains an assumption.
3. **Choose:** explain one priority and one tradeoff in plain language.
4. **Coordinate:** make the next action clear to each teammate or audience.
5. **Check:** define what observation or metric could show whether the change helped.
6. **Revise:** record what changed after feedback and what is still unknown.
