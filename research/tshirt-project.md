## What the existing research observed

The report compares different buying models, not interchangeable T-shirt brands:

- **UNIQLO:** direct apparel retail. The captured page showed product-first presentation, color/size controls, a size guide, price and add-to-cart; a Bestseller label and review count were visible social-proof cues. An add-to-cart attempt was interrupted, so the final cart/checkout path was not observed.
- **Michaels / Gildan:** a craft retailer's listing for a Gildan blank shirt. The captured size-L cart showed $3.29. This is evidence about Michaels' listing and cart, not Gildan's own store.
- **Custom Ink:** customized apparel and group-order tooling. The report observed a blank-shirt design flow and choices around shipping, collecting sizes, and group payment. A captured starting price was $7.75 per item, not a final quote.
- **Gildan's U.S. site:** access was denied during the capture, so the report does not claim a direct Gildan website review.

The report also includes a separate Stüssy fit-reference note. It describes one other shirt and is not evidence for the learner's product fit.

The displayed values above are dated snapshots from different products and order paths. Prices, offers, stock, ratings, and page behavior can change. No order was placed and final shipping/payment checkout was not completed.

## Design and persuasion lenses

The report uses archetypes, Cialdini's principles, and modernist/postmodernist tendencies as **interpretive lenses** on visible interfaces. It does not claim that companies self-identify with these labels or that a cue caused sales.

- UNIQLO's restrained, product-first page is read as modernist-leaning; the observed rating and bestseller marker are social proof cues.
- Michaels presented a practical catalog with promotional layers; the report says a sale banner alone is not enough to assign a persuasion principle.
- Custom Ink combines a guided order interface with open-ended user design. A rating and popularity markers are visible social proof. Voluntarily investing in the design tool may be a possible consistency mechanism, not proof of intent or effect.

These observations can prompt product questions: what detail does a shopper need before choosing a size, what is included in a customization offer, and how can a page stay easy to scan without overwhelming the product?

| Testing | The PR description reports automated checks for its own branch. | Attribute reported checks to that PR. A passing check is not evidence of market demand, fit, or customer satisfaction. |

## Honest next evidence to gather

1. Ask a small number of classmates to complete one simple page-finding task and describe where they hesitate. Record the exact task and observations; do not claim statistical significance.
2. Ask what they believe the shirt allows them to do after seeing the page. Compare their understanding with the product facts that can actually be verified.
3. Separate an observed behavior from an interpretation: “three people missed the size guide” is an observation if it happened; “people prefer the modernist design” is a broader claim that needs more evidence.
4. Record one revision and why it follows from the feedback. Keep the current Brand Studio issue/PR as the source of truth for that project's decisions.

The learner's current interest in Product Management fits the research, prioritization, communication, and feedback loop illustrated here. FDE comparison would need additional technical implementation and evaluation evidence; this project alone does not show production AI engineering.
